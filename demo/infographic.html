Cursus

A course planner whose tools refuse, say what a choice closes off two years before it bites, hold a limit you set against the agent, and can be rewound. Built on WebMCP. A worked example, not a product for one university.

Every refusal has the same shape

A tool that only fails leaves the agent reporting failure. One that names the rule, the reason and the remedy leaves it repairing the situation — and in our tests, asking before it applied the fix.

Refused. ADV-301 (Advanced Calculus) requires CALC-102 (Calculus II) and NUM-201 (Numerical Methods), and NUM-201 (Numerical Methods) is not in the plan. To unblock it: place NUM-201 in a term before 5. Rule: PREREQ_NOT_MET. The plan was not changed.
the reason — what the rules found the remedy — what would unblock it the rule — which one, by name

9 rules belong to the university. One belongs to the student.

UNKNOWN_COURSEALREADY_IN_PLANNO_SUCH_TERMNOT_OFFERED_IN_TERMPREREQ_NOT_METSCHEDULE_CLASHCREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDEDNOT_IN_PLANDEPENDENT_IN_PLANPROTECTED_TRACK

A prerequisite, a clash, a credit cap — those are the same for everyone. protect_track is the student's own: say do not close Graphics and Animation, and from then on anything that would close it is refused, including when you ask for it yourself an hour later. The refusal cites your instruction, not the handbook.

One decision, and the rest falls out

a tool call→the rules accept it→an event is appended →state = reduce(log)
undo_to(n) is reduce(log.slice(0, n))
list_actions() is the log itself
share_plan() is the log in a link, replayed through these same rules
a refusal is the reducer never being reached. Nothing is written.

Adapting it to another institution

Two files. catalogue.js is the courses, terms and specialisations; rules.js is what that university will not allow. The 13 tools, the event log, the shape of a refusal and the layer that holds a student's own limits against the agent do not change.

The catalogue is invented and no registrar has seen it. Every figure on this page was read out of the code when the page was written, and the refusal above is the string the tool returns — demo/infographic.py regenerates it. Nothing here was transcribed by hand.
